Why a church for 55 and better?

The largest generation in American history is aging into a country that quietly stopped building things for it.

Most churches aim at young families. Fair enough. But that leaves millions of people, with more wisdom, time, and freedom than they've ever had, sitting out the most important conversation of their lives. Grey Wave is built around this generation on purpose, from day one.

What we're about

Made on purpose. Made for a purpose. And not finished yet.

1

You were designed, not manufactured.

Your wiring, your history, the things you can't help caring about. None of it was an accident, and none of it expires. We'll help you name what you're made of and put it to work.

2

A table, not an audience.

Friends who notice when you're missing, bring soup when you're sick, and tell you the truth when you need it. That is what church was always supposed to be.

3

Jesus, told plainly.

No guilt trip, no fine print. The story that has held people together for two thousand years, explained the way you'd explain it to a friend at breakfast.

A Sunday, minute by minute

Here's exactly what happens, so there are no surprises.

Before it starts

Coffee's on. Someone's at the door.

Pull into close parking, walk in a level entrance, and a person with a name tag will say hello, hand you a coffee, and show you where the restrooms are. That's it. You can sit wherever you like, including the back.

Minute 0

A few songs you already know.

Hymns and songs most people over fifty can sing without looking, played at a volume that invites you in instead of drowning you out. The words are on the screen in large type. Sit or stand, sing or don't.

Minute 10

Lance talks for about twenty-five minutes.

One idea from the Bible, explained in plain English, with a story or two and something you can actually do with it this week. Clear sound, printed notes if you want them, and no one will ask you to read out loud.

Minute 40

Communion: bread and juice, passed down the row.

Christians have done this every week for two thousand years to remember Jesus. We'll explain it in a sentence each time. If you follow Jesus, take it. If you're still deciding, pass the tray along. Nobody is watching who does what.

Minute 55

You're done. Really.

No one will single you out, ask you to stand, or hand you a card to fill in. If you'd like someone to know you were there, say so. If you'd rather slip out to the car, that's fine too. We hope you'll be back.

Afterward

Stay for a refill and a real conversation.

Most people stick around. Nobody sits alone, and nobody gets cornered. This is usually where the church actually happens, and where the lunch plans get made.

Who's behind this

Lance has spent 45 years starting churches for other people. This one is for his own generation.

Lance Hurley planted his first church in 1981 with twenty people in Manteno, Illinois. He has started two more since, and today he leads Ignite Church Planting, which has helped launch forty churches across Chicagoland. Along the way he watched too many friends drift from church in retirement because nothing new was ever built for them.

So he and Darla, married 42 years, decided to build one. Not a program inside someone else's church. A church.

Honest questions

The things people ask before they'll come.

I'm not religious. Is this going to be awkward?

No. You'll hear about Jesus, because that's what a church is for, but nobody will corner you, quiz you, or ask you to raise a hand. Curious is enough. Plenty of people in the room are still working it out.

I got hurt by a church once. Why would this be different?

It might not be, and we'd rather say so than promise otherwise. What we can promise: the people leading this have spent a lifetime in churches and have seen the damage too. No pressure, no manipulation, no secrets about money. If we get something wrong, tell Lance to his face.

Do I have to give money?

No. We don't pass a plate and nobody will ask. The church is paid for by people who've decided to be part of it. If that's ever you, there's a page for it. If it isn't, you're just as welcome.

Is this a nursing-home kind of thing?

Not remotely. It's a church for people with more freedom than they've had since they were twenty, and more to offer than anyone's asked for. Walkers and wheelchairs are welcome. So are motorcycles.

I don't hear or see as well as I used to.

Clear sound, words on a big screen in large type, printed notes, and a text-size button on this website. Tell the person at the door and they'll put you where it works best.

What if I don't believe what you believe?

Then we'll have something to talk about. We'll be honest about what we think is true, and we expect you to be honest back. Nobody has ever been argued into faith, so we won't try.

Can I bring my grandkids? My dog? My skeptical husband?

Yes, yes if he's well behaved, and especially yes.
