What to expect

An hour that feels like it was built for you. Because it was.

Coffee first

Doors open 30 minutes early. Grab a cup and sit anywhere. The front rows aren't reserved for anybody.

Songs you know

Hymns and the songs of the last fifty years, at a pitch and volume you can actually join. Words in large print, every time.

One clear talk

One passage of the Bible, explained plainly and applied to real life: grief, grandkids, health, money, regret, and what comes next. About 25 minutes.

Communion, then conversation

Bread and juice, passed down the row, every week. Take it if you follow Jesus, pass it along if you're still deciding. Then we stay and talk.

Good to know

The practical stuff, handled.

  • About an hour, start to finish
  • Daytime, so no driving after dark
  • Level entrance, close parking, wide aisles
  • Large print and clear sound
  • No offering plate passed
  • Nobody singled out, ever
  • Wear whatever you'd wear to the diner
  • Rides available if you ask

Questions people ask

The honest answers.

Do I have to dress up?

No. Sunday clothes, gardening clothes, whatever you'd wear to the diner. You will not be underdressed.

I don't hear well. Will I be able to follow?

Yes. We keep the music at a reasonable volume, the speaking clear, and the words on screen in large print. If you use hearing aids with a telecoil, tell a greeter and we'll help.

I use a walker or a wheelchair.

The entrance is level, the parking is close, and there is space at the end of the rows. You won't be a bother. You'll be a regular.

I'm not religious. Or I was raised something else.

You're welcome. Catholic, Lutheran, Baptist, Methodist, lapsed, or "never went." Nobody will quiz you, ask you to stand up, or expect you to know when to sit.

My spouse passed away. I don't want to walk in alone.

Then don't. Check the box on the form and someone will meet you in the parking lot and sit with you. Plenty of people here are walking the same road. Grief has a seat here.

Will you ask me for money?

No. There is no plate passed and nobody will call about giving. If you decide later that you want to help, the Give page explains how. That's your move, not ours.

Is it only for older people?

Grey Wave is built for people 55 and better, but the door is open to anyone. Grandkids, adult children, the neighbor who's forty. Bring whoever you want.

Is this a real church or a program at another church?

A real church, with its own people and, soon, its own leaders. We're in a preview season right now, meeting in a sister church's building while we get started. You're early, which means you get a say in what this becomes.

Something else? Ask us and a real person will answer.

We'll save you a seat.

Tell us you're coming and your first Sunday gets a lot easier.
